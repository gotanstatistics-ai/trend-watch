Tesla's new Roadster jumped, flipped and drove upside down. Here is how it actually does it

• Tanks of compressed air feed cold-gas thrusters: four at the rear give about 80 kN of push, with more on the sides and underneath for jumps.
• Fans and skirts suck the car onto the road, the trick that let it drive up a wall and along the ceiling of a loop.
• Claimed 0 to 60 mph in 0.81 seconds and a top speed above 230 mph, but only on the one-seat Rocket trim, which is not road legal.
• Open questions: how long the air lasts, the safety of the gas plumes, and whether a human can take that acceleration.

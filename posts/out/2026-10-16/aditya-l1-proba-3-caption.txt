India's Aditya-L1 and Europe's Proba-3 watched the Sun erupt together for the first time

• Two solar eruptions last September were caught by both spacecraft at once: one racing out at 645 km per second, the other at 145.
• Proba-3 makes an artificial eclipse: two satellites fly 150 metres apart, one blocking the Sun so the other can see its faint outer atmosphere.
• Aditya-L1 reads the light itself to measure temperature and speed, so together they show how an eruption forms and grows.
• These eruptions are what disturb satellites and power grids when they head for Earth. The study was led from Bengaluru.

Source: IIA Bengaluru, Astronomy & Astrophysics
